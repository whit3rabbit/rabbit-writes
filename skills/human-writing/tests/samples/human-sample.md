Solar got cheap faster than anyone at the utility planned for.

Module prices fell about 90% between 2010 and 2023. I remember sitting in a resource-planning meeting in 2014 where a consultant put up a curve showing solar reaching parity around 2035, and half the room thought he was being optimistic. He was off by eleven years in the wrong direction.

What changed wasn't the panels, exactly. Panels got better, sure, but the bigger shift was that installers stopped losing money on soft costs. Permitting, racking, sales, financing. In Germany a residential rooftop install ran roughly half the US price for most of the 2010s, and almost none of that gap was hardware.

Storage is where it still gets messy. Lithium prices swung hard in 2022 and again last year, and the four-hour battery that pencils out in California does not pencil out in Minnesota in February. Anyone who tells you the storage problem is solved has not looked at a winter load curve.

I don't think the grid people were stupid. They were extrapolating from a technology that had behaved a certain way for thirty years, and it stopped behaving that way. That's a hard thing to see from inside.

The part I still can't square: we knew the learning rate. Swanson's law was published in 2006. We had the number and we discounted it anyway.
