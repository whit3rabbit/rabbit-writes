# Form: linkedin

**Register:** `linkedin`

A form file supplies slots. Only the voice may fill them. Every quoted phrase in this file is under Tells, and every one of them is a phrase to avoid.

The one form with its own register rather than a rung on the formality spine, because its tolerances come from the platform and not from how formal the writing is. A feed truncates the post after about three lines behind a see-more control, there is no title, and the typography conventions are local to the platform.

## Slots

- **Hook.** The first two or three lines, which are the only ones a scrolling reader sees. It carries the claim or the specific detail. Everything the reader needs to decide whether to expand sits here.
- **Body.** Short paragraphs with blank lines between them, because the platform renders them tightly.
- **Close.** The point, or the ask. One of them, not both.
- **Hashtags.** At most a few, at the end, on their own line. The register relaxes hashtag stuffing nowhere: `formal` marks it extra-strict and every other register runs it at full strength, so this is a slot with a small budget rather than a free one.

## Bands

| Purpose | Band |
|---|---|
| A note, one thought | under 150 words |
| A standard post | 150 to 300 words |
| Past 300 words | it wanted to be an article, and the platform has a separate surface for that |

## Where this register's tolerances come from

The matrix relaxes four things here and each has a platform reason, not a stylistic one.

- **Em dashes, two per post.** Plain text with no italics available, so the dash does work that other formatting would do elsewhere.
- **Bold on hooks.** Same reason, and the hook is the only line most readers see.
- **Emoji at the end of a heading, one or two.** The local convention, and it is a convention this register tolerates rather than one it recommends. A voice that forbids emoji still forbids them here, because a register never relaxes a voice rule.
- **One rhetorical question, and subjectless fragments.** Both are the register.

The matrix also skips several rules here that fire elsewhere: excessive bullets, copula avoidance, uniform paragraph length, transition phrases, generic conclusions, and tier-3 vocabulary density. Those are skipped because the form is short and structurally repetitive by design, and reporting them would report the form itself.

`forms/substack.md` explains why a newsletter does not inherit any of this.

## Tells

Phrases and shapes named here are the ones to avoid. Nothing in this file is a phrase to use.

- "I'm humbled to announce" and "Thrilled to share."
- "Agree?" as a closing line.
- "Most people get this wrong." and "Nobody talks about this."
- A one-line-per-paragraph ladder running the whole post. The register tolerates the shape at the hook and not for twelve lines.
- A story that exists only to reach a business lesson, where the story did not happen the way it is told. That is the guardrail against inventing facts, and it is the single most common failure in this form.
- Hashtags in the middle of a sentence.
- A question in the hook that the post never answers.

## What the mechanical layer sees here

A post at 150 to 300 words sits right at the reliability floor, so the stylometrics are thin and should be read as thin. The relaxed cells above mean a scan of a LinkedIn post reports much less than the same text scanned as a blog post, which is correct and is also why a post that still reports a craft finding under this register is worth taking seriously: it cleared a bar deliberately set low.

Promotional language is relaxed by one hit here, on the grounds that some selling is the point of the form. The second one is reported.
